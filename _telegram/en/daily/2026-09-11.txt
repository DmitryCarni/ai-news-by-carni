AI Daily — September 11, 2026

AI infrastructure demand is already measured in hundreds of billions of dollars, while enterprise agents are starting to work for days and weeks instead of one chat at a time.

Oracle's remaining performance obligations reached $664B. During the quarter it booked more than $30B in new AI cloud contracts, brought 850 MW of additional data-center capacity online and delivered more than 300,000 GPUs to AI cloud customers. Oracle says demand for AI training and inference still exceeds supply.

Salesforce showed the other side of the shift: seven job-ready agents across sales, service, commerce, employee operations and the back office, plus a new long-horizon runtime. Agents can preserve state, keep working across days or weeks and adapt their plan as new information arrives.

OpenAI, according to Bloomberg and Reuters, is open to slowing frontier AI development and potentially coordinating pace with other labs. This is not a pause announcement, but development speed itself is becoming a strategic safety variable.

Infrastructure capital keeps moving downstream too. Positron AI raised $875M at a $5B valuation for memory-first inference hardware and software — another sign that the economics of running models is becoming its own major market.

In finance, Accenture launched Trusted Wealth Ops on Salesforce and Claude. Reasoning is separated from governed data, permissions, deterministic execution, compliance controls and human approval — a more realistic architecture for AI working around money.

Product opportunities:
• long-running agents for one end-to-end business process;
• memory, permissions, audit and checkpoints for those agents;
• inference-cost and infrastructure optimization.
