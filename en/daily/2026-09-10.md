## Fundamental trends {#fundamental}

### Visa, Mastercard and Ant International start building a shared identity layer for AI agents

On September 10, Ant International, Mastercard and Visa announced work on a Know-Your-Agent (KYA) interoperability framework for identifying and verifying AI agents that make purchases on users’ behalf. The goal is to let card networks, wallets, agent platforms and marketplaces recognize trusted agents across ecosystems while keeping their own approval and risk controls.

This matters because the payments industry is moving beyond the question of whether an agent can recommend a product. It is preparing for agents that can actually initiate purchases. KYA is designed to connect existing mechanisms including Visa Trusted Agent Protocol, Mastercard Verifiable Intent and Ant International’s Agentic Mobile Protocol.

The market signal is broader than payments: agent identity is becoming infrastructure. At scale, autonomous commerce will need verifiable agent identity, proof of user intent, bounded permissions, action logs and clear accountability for transactions.

For builders, this creates demand for agent identity, authorization, intent verification, fraud controls and payment integration. Prototypes are possible today; broad commercial value depends on interoperability standards gaining adoption.

### Specialized accelerators are moving inside NVIDIA’s architecture instead of replacing it outright

d-Matrix said on September 10 that its next-generation Raptor XPUs for inference will connect to NVIDIA infrastructure through NVLink Fusion. The company plans to use NVLink, MGX, Spectrum-X and other NVIDIA platform components to build rack-scale systems that mix specialized accelerators with GPUs.

Architecturally, that is a meaningful shift. The custom-chip market increasingly looks less like “NVIDIA versus everyone else” and more like heterogeneous compute inside an NVIDIA-defined rack and networking stack. For chip startups, that reduces the infrastructure they must build themselves; for NVIDIA, it extends the platform’s reach even when some workloads run on third-party silicon.

NVIDIA says the current NVLink Fusion stack can deliver up to 3 TB/s of all-to-all bandwidth per XPU, three times lower XPU-to-XPU latency than off-the-shelf Ethernet and ten times higher packet rates. Those are vendor claims for the platform, not independent benchmarks of a finished d-Matrix system. Raptor-based systems are expected in 2027.

## Where AI becomes a product {#applied}

### NVIDIA and Palantir start with NVIDIA’s own supply chain

NVIDIA and Palantir announced a joint stack for critical supply chains on September 10, combining NVIDIA Nemotron open models with Palantir Foundry, AIP and Ontology. The first announced deployment is NVIDIA’s own supply chain, where the system is intended to surface constraints, codify operating knowledge and guide decisions across the path from wafer production to usable compute capacity.

That is more meaningful than a generic partnership announcement. One of the world’s most complex and capacity-constrained AI supply chains is becoming the proving ground for an agentic operating stack. If the deployment produces measurable gains, it would strengthen the case for AI as an operating layer for physical production rather than just office productivity.

The sellable product is not a chatbot sitting on top of ERP. It is a combination of enterprise data modeling, operational ontology, models, permissions and actions connected to production systems. A narrow MVP can be built in months; end-to-end supply-chain transformation remains a multi-year program.

### IBM and NASA release a lunar foundation model with an open multimodal dataset

IBM and NASA released the NASA-IBM Lunar Foundation Model on September 10 together with a machine-learning-ready lunar dataset containing more than 30 spatially aligned layers from nine instruments across four missions. The model is designed for tasks including lunar ice detection, crater analysis and volcanic-feature mapping.

In results published by IBM and NASA, the model reduced error in identifying high-potential ice areas by up to 22% versus SwinV2-B and improved context-scale crater detection by nearly 19% while using half the training data. Those performance figures come from the model authors and associated technical work; Reuters independently confirmed the release and intended use.

The wider signal is the repeatable pattern behind scientific AI: a domain foundation model plus a well-curated multimodal dataset that can support several downstream tasks. The same architecture is relevant to geology, climate, industrial inspection, medicine and other data-rich scientific fields.

## Architecture and deployment {#stack}

### Enterprise AI is converging on governed action rather than unconstrained autonomy

Several September 10 announcements point to the same deployment pattern. KYA in payments requires agent identity and user-intent verification; NVIDIA and Palantir tie models to an enterprise ontology and controlled operating environment; Ripple Treasury requires agents to cite policy and wait for human approval before execution.

The emerging architecture is stable enough to matter: the model recommends or orchestrates an action, while identity, permissions, policies, logging and approval live in a separate control layer. For money, contracts, industrial systems and external actions, this is a much more practical enterprise design than unrestricted agent autonomy.

For platform teams and integrators, the new baseline includes an agent registry, roles and permissions, policy enforcement, approval for critical actions, event logging and rapid access revocation. A first version for one workflow is realistic in one to three months.

## Finance {#finance}

### Treasury AI gets policies, evidence and mandatory human approval

Ripple Treasury expanded GSmart on September 10, adding AI capabilities across forecasting, liquidity, risk, reconciliation and reporting. The company says its agents monitor processes, propose a specific action, cite the policy clause behind that action and wait for human approval before anything executes.

Knowledge Studio serves as a separate governance layer that checks proposed actions against organizational policies before escalating them to a person. That is the important design choice for finance: value comes not from a polished model response, but from safely embedding AI inside a process that can move or expose money.

Ripple says 60% of eligible customers have enabled Risk Insights and 44% use Forecast Insights. Those are vendor-reported adoption figures rather than independent measurements, but they are still a stronger commercial signal than a pilot with no production usage.

The near-term market is cash-flow forecasting, anomaly detection, reconciliation, liquidity decisions and exception handling with explicit human approval. Starting with recommendations and exceptions is far more realistic than giving an agent autonomous control of funds.

## Business opportunities {#monetization}

### A trust-and-permissions layer for enterprise AI agents

KYA and governed finance agents point to a concrete product category: software that knows which agent is acting, on whose behalf, with what permissions, under which policy, and which actions require human confirmation. This can be sold as part of enterprise-agent deployment without building a proprietary LLM.

A first MVP can be an agent registry, role model, allowlist of actions, audit log and approval workflow for one business process. Finance, procurement, support and internal operations are practical starting points, with scope expanding as the number of agents grows.
