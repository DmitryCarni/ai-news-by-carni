## Fundamental trends {#fundamental}

### Salesforce turns 27 years of CRM expertise into a specialized reasoning model

Salesforce and NVIDIA introduced Koa on September 15, Salesforce's first CRM reasoning model. The important part is not another model launch but how it was built: NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Super was post-trained on synthetic scenarios modeled on nearly three decades of Salesforce CRM deployments.

The scenarios span more than 14 industries and teach the model to execute sequences of business actions — qualifying leads, updating opportunities, routing cases, selecting tools and completing multistep workflows. Salesforce says Koa matches or exceeds leading models on CRM actions in its own benchmark while making three times fewer errors. That remains a vendor benchmark and has not yet been independently validated.

Salesforce controls the weights and runs post-training and inference inside its own infrastructure. No customer data was used to train Koa, and Salesforce says customer data stays inside its trust boundary during inference. Koa is already used internally and is moving into pilots with Formula 1, UChicago Medicine, Xero, 1-800Accountant and others, with U.S. general availability expected in winter 2026.

This points to a broader market shift: instead of using one general frontier model for every workflow, software vendors can combine a strong open-weight base model with proprietary domain knowledge and a controlled execution environment. Decades of accumulated workflow expertise are becoming model-building assets.

### Europe expands the inference accelerator market beyond NVIDIA

Dutch startup Axelera AI launched Europa, its second-generation accelerator, and announced supply contracts for AI factories. Unlike its first-generation Metis chips, which focused on edge deployments, Europa targets heavier enterprise server workloads and is set to appear in selected Dell and Supermicro systems.

Reuters reports that Axelera now has more than 600 customers, has signed deals worth tens of millions of dollars and is pursuing a potential sales pipeline of up to $1.5 billion. That last figure is not booked orders or revenue. On the same day, fellow Dutch startup Euclyd announced a funding round of more than €200 million for energy-efficient AI infrastructure.

The signal is not that NVIDIA's leadership is suddenly disappearing. The inference market is becoming large enough for specialized accelerators to win real server integrations, European AI-factory deployments and substantial capital. As models run continuously at scale, execution cost, power efficiency and hardware choice matter more.

## Where AI becomes a product {#applied}

### Adecco is rolling an AI coworker out to 27,000 employees across 40+ countries

The Adecco Group announced a global rollout of Agentforce Coworker across more than 40 countries after pilots in the UK and France. The Claude-powered system inside Salesforce is moving into the daily work of 27,000 employees across sales, recruitment and client/candidate engagement.

This is no longer a local chatbot. Coworker provides one interface to data, systems and organizational knowledge that had been spread across dozens of tools. Recruiters can select a candidate and launch pre-screening, recruiter or onboarding agents; sales teams can identify priority prospects, prepare briefs and enrich lead data. The system can draw on context from more than 2.5 million agent-candidate interactions since April 2025.

The scale matters. Adecco already runs agentic recruitment workflows in ten countries representing 50% of Adecco business revenue. Moving the system to 27,000 employees is a shift from proving that agents work to making them part of the operating model of a global company.

## Architecture and deployment {#stack}

### The enterprise stack is splitting into base model, domain model and trust boundary

Koa offers a concrete blueprint for enterprise AI architecture. A general open-weight model supplies broad capability; post-training adds domain logic; owned infrastructure keeps weights, data and inference inside a controlled environment; an agent layer then invokes tools and executes business actions.

Salesforce is extending the same pattern into Missionforce for government and regulated organizations, including private clouds and air-gapped networks. The engineering question is becoming more precise than simply “cloud or local”: which parts of intelligence, data and execution does an organization need to control directly?

For integrators, this lowers the bar for specialization. They do not need to train a foundation model from scratch. The valuable layer can be a domain task set, evaluations, tool contracts, permissions and a secure execution environment that turns a capable general model into a reliable specialist.

## Finance {#finance}

### Workiva gives finance and compliance teams a governed agent builder

Workiva introduced Agent Studio, a no-code environment for building, customizing and deploying AI agents inside its financial reporting, audit, risk and compliance platform. Organizations can enrich agents with company knowledge, adapt them to internal processes and schedule recurring work.

The execution environment matters more than the no-code label. Workiva operates in audit-ready and regulated workflows, where teams need to trace data, actions and the basis for an output. The company also expanded AI capabilities for regulatory preparation and reporting work.

This reinforces a pattern visible across regulated AI: the commercial sweet spot is governed automation rather than maximum autonomy. A practical finance MVP is an agent that gathers data, performs repeatable checks, prepares a document or reconciliation and leaves an auditable trail for a human reviewer, auditor or regulator.

## Business opportunities {#monetization}

### A domain AI layer on top of existing ERP and CRM systems is becoming a product category

Koa makes a broadly reusable commercial idea visible. Companies already own domain logic: approval routes, exceptions, order-handling rules, accounting controls and sales or support playbooks. Those can be converted into evaluated tasks and tool contracts for specialized agents without building a foundation model.

For a smaller integrator, a realistic MVP is one expensive workflow with a measurable outcome: an agent reads ERP or CRM data, chooses from permitted actions, prepares a decision and leaves the critical step to a human. The product is not “AI” in the abstract; it is shorter cycle time and fewer errors in a specific process. Well-documented domain logic becomes the key asset.

## Day in review

- **Enterprise AI is specializing.** Koa shifts the center of gravity from a universal model toward domain models carrying a software vendor's accumulated business expertise.
- **Agents are moving from pilots to organizational scale.** Adecco's 27,000 employees across 40+ countries is an operating-model deployment, not a team experiment.
- **Control of the stack is becoming a product feature.** Weights, data, inference, tools and execution environments are increasingly designed as separate governed layers.
- **Inference economics are opening room for new accelerators.** European chipmakers are not replacing NVIDIA, but they are winning server integrations, AI-factory contracts and serious capital.
