## Fundamental trends {#fundamental}

### Claude now leads 26% of the work building Anthropic’s next generation of AI

Anthropic has published one of the clearest measurements yet of AI being used to build future AI systems. As of August, Claude “leads” 26% of the company’s AI R&D work — meaning it can complete most of a task end-to-end from a high-level prompt while a human supervises. More than 90% of Anthropic’s research work is now at least at the “AI collaborates” level. None of the measured areas is fully autonomous yet.

The scale of the internal agent system is equally notable. Around 30,000 agents were doing research and engineering work at any one time on Anthropic’s main internal platform. Every action passes through an online monitor before execution. Across more than a billion decisions in August, about 0.002% — roughly one in 47,000 — were blocked.

The important shift is from speculation about AI self-improvement to a measurable production process. Anthropic is explicit that 26% does not mean an AI is autonomously building its successor. But as AI itself accelerates R&D, the rate of model progress increasingly depends on agent oversight, permissions and the ability to control thousands of parallel workers — not compute alone.

## Where AI becomes a product {#applied}

### Google turns CC from a personal assistant into a shared household agent

Google Labs has expanded its experimental CC agent from a single-user assistant into an agent for families and households. CC now gets its own verified Google Account and a distinct permissions model. Up to six people can collaborate with one agent, while each member decides which emails, files and calendars to share.

CC produces a shared morning brief, maintains Calendar and Tasks, extracts dates from shared material, can pre-fill registration PDFs, and can create shopping lists and meal plans. Each CC instance runs on an isolated cloud computer powered by Google’s Antigravity agent harness and Gemini models. Actions or information sharing outside the group require permission.

The product signal is bigger than a family chatbot. The agent has its own identity, persistent shared memory and per-person access boundaries. That starts to look like a digital participant in a group rather than an interface owned by one user. CC remains an early Google Labs experiment for U.S. adults with personal Google accounts and is currently waitlisted.

## Architecture and deployment {#stack}

### Optical connectivity is becoming a bottleneck inside AI data centers

GlobalFoundries and Marvell expanded a multi-year agreement to increase manufacturing capacity for silicon-germanium (SiGe) technology at GlobalFoundries’ Vermont facility. Marvell needs the additional supply for optical transceivers, near-packaged optics and co-packaged optics used to move data at high speed inside AI data centers.

The signal is that the infrastructure race is moving below the GPU layer. As more accelerators are connected into a single compute system, bandwidth, latency and the energy cost of moving data increasingly constrain useful performance. Capital therefore flows not only into accelerators but into photonics, packaging and specialized networking components.

That broadens the AI infrastructure value chain. Companies do not need to train models or manufacture GPUs to benefit from the buildout if they remove bottlenecks between compute nodes.

## Finance {#finance}

### Small-business AI underwriting moves beyond reading bank statements

U.S. lender Byzfunder launched TraceDataIQ, an in-house underwriting intelligence platform for small-business finance. The company says the system analyzes a business’s broader financial, operational and public profile rather than stopping at bank-statement extraction. It is built on performance history from more than $2 billion in financing across over 35,000 businesses.

The direction matters: financial AI is moving from document extraction toward combining multiple data classes into a decision-ready view of how a business actually operates. That can reduce manual information gathering and give underwriters a more complete risk picture, although the performance claims currently come from the vendor and still need independent validation on real portfolios.

For banks and non-bank lenders, the hard questions are increasingly architectural: which sources may be used, how the assessment can be explained, where automated decision-making must stop, and whether the result can be reproduced for audit.

## Business opportunities {#monetization}

### Controlling thousands of agents is becoming an infrastructure product of its own

Anthropic’s 30,000 concurrent internal agents point to the next layer of enterprise AI infrastructure: identity for every agent, permissions, action logs, automated blocking, post-action review and escalation to humans. Companies will need this well before they reach frontier-lab scale — even dozens of agents touching code, files, ERP systems or external services create a meaningful control problem.

A practical product is an agent control layer with unified audit logs, action policies, limits, risk checks and a clear human-approval path. An MVP can start with one action class, such as ERP writes or infrastructure commands, rather than trying to govern every agent behavior at once.

### Shared agents create a new B2B product pattern

Google CC’s design transfers naturally from households to small teams: a distinct agent identity, persistent memory, per-member permissions and a shared output. That is a useful pattern for project teams, small departments and administrative workflows where context is currently scattered across email, calendars and files.

The sellable outcome is not “LLM access” but less coordination work. A realistic MVP is one team agent that reads only approved sources, maintains shared commitments and produces a daily brief without permission to execute high-risk actions on its own.
