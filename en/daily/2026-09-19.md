## Fundamental trends {#fundamental}

### Anthropic weighs a new model just after calling for the AI race to slow down

Reuters reported, citing three sources, that Anthropic is considering releasing a new model in response to OpenAI's momentum after GPT-6 Astra. No launch decision has been announced; one source said the company is separately evaluating the safety of its next model. The timing is striking because Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called only a week earlier for the industry to slow the growth of frontier-model capabilities.

Competitive pressure helps explain the tension. Ramp data cited by Reuters puts Astra at roughly 13% of tracked enterprise AI spending versus about 8% for Claude Fable. OpenRouter also recorded the first week in more than two and a half years in which users spent more on OpenAI models than on Anthropic models. Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate exceeded $65 billion by the end of July, while OpenAI's passed $40 billion.

This is not a model announcement, nor proof that Anthropic has abandoned its safety position. It is a unusually clear coordination problem: even a lab publicly arguing for a slower pace can lose market share if it slows down alone. Without common rules, AI safety carries a direct competitive cost as well as a technical one.

### China starts treating AI as a possible macroeconomic imbalance amplifier

Huang Yiping, a member of the People's Bank of China's monetary policy committee, warned at a Beijing economic forum that wider AI adoption could deepen China's existing imbalance between strong supply and weak domestic demand. In his view, the global AI boom is helping exports, but productivity growth without stronger consumption could make the structural mismatch more persistent.

That broadens the macroeconomic AI debate. Most discussion has focused on productivity, investment and compute demand; a major policy voice is now explicitly asking how the gains are distributed. If AI raises production faster than household income and consumption, automation can increase pressure on prices, employment and capacity utilization even while improving efficiency.

For businesses, this is not a crisis forecast but a planning signal: AI efficiency cannot be valued only through lower unit costs. In demand-constrained markets, creating demand, increasing revenue per customer and reallocating capacity faster may matter as much as producing more cheaply.

## Architecture and deployment {#stack}

### Codex fixes reasoning-summary compatibility with external providers

OpenAI released Codex CLI 0.155.1, restoring a safer default for new local TUI sessions: reasoning summaries are disabled unless explicitly configured. The prior behavior could cause requests to be rejected by model providers that do not support the reasoning-summary field. Explicit user settings remain respected.

The patch is small but architecturally useful. Agent tools increasingly sit above multiple models, gateways and providers. A single protocol field can break the chain. Production agent stacks therefore need capability detection, conservative defaults and tests for the full client-to-gateway-to-provider path rather than only the model itself.

- The strongest signal is measurable tension between safety and competition: calls to slow down now collide directly with enterprise market-share pressure.
- AI is entering macroeconomic policy not only as a productivity engine but as a potential amplifier of the gap between production and demand.
- In deployment, maturity increasingly depends on whether agent infrastructure can survive differences between providers and protocols, not merely on model capability.
