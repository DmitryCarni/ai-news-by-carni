## Fundamental trends {#fundamental}

### Microsoft researchers use AI to improve weather forecasts up to six weeks ahead

On October 14, the scientific journal Nature published a study by an international research team including Microsoft Research scientists on improving weather forecasts two to six weeks into the future. The researchers introduced Probabilistic Bias Correction (PBC), a machine-learning framework designed to reduce systematic errors in weather prediction. Rather than attempting to replace established meteorological systems with an entirely new neural network, the method studies mistakes in previous forecasts and adjusts the probability estimates produced by existing models. This approach addresses a particularly difficult forecasting window. Both traditional physics-based systems and modern AI models lose considerable skill between two and six weeks ahead as errors accumulate and atmospheric, oceanic and land processes interact. The October 14 event is the publication of the findings in Nature, rather than the first appearance of every underlying research result.

The study brought together researchers from Microsoft Research and several external scientific institutions. They evaluated PBC using forecasting systems operated by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF), including conventional dynamical models and AI-based approaches. According to the published results, PBC improved forecasts relative to ECMWF's operational bias-correction procedure for 98% of evaluated precipitation targets, 92% of temperature targets and 91% of atmospheric-pressure targets. When applied to ECMWF's AI Forecasting System, the framework also doubled its previously modest subseasonal forecast skill under the study's evaluation conditions. These percentages must not be interpreted as the probability that an individual forecast is correct or as a corresponding percentage reduction in all weather errors. They refer to the proportion of evaluated targets or locations showing improvements in the researchers' statistical performance measures.

Technically, PBC operates on probabilistic forecast distributions. A weather model might estimate the likelihood of different temperatures or rainfall amounts several weeks ahead. The framework examines historical forecasts and subsequent observations to identify persistent distortions in those probability distributions. Its Debias++ and Persistence++ components generate complementary corrections, which are then combined into a refined probability forecast. This allows the system to adjust not only the expected value of a meteorological variable but also the spread and shape of the forecast distribution. Unlike a large general-purpose generative model, the method is designed to improve the outputs of existing forecasting systems rather than rebuild the entire meteorological prediction process. Its relatively lightweight computational requirements could make it accessible to institutions without the resources to operate major supercomputing facilities.

The researchers also tested the approach in the ECMWF AI Weather Quest competition, held in 2025. In the Nature publication, they report that their PBC-based forecasting system achieved the best results across the evaluated weather variables and forecast lead times. The entry outperformed dynamical systems from six operational forecasting centers, an international multimodel ensemble, ECMWF's AI Forecasting System and other participating teams. This provides evidence beyond a single retrospective comparison. Nevertheless, success in a forecasting competition does not mean that the method has already been deployed universally by national weather services. Its real-world performance still depends on the quality of the underlying models, the availability of historical observations and regional climate characteristics. Independent operational evaluation remains particularly important for rare and extreme weather events, where inaccurate predictions can have significant consequences.

The fundamental significance of the research is its demonstration that AI can improve established scientific systems by learning to correct their recurring weaknesses. Progress does not always require building a larger model or purchasing more computing capacity. In some applications, a comparatively lightweight learning algorithm can extract additional value from existing scientific infrastructure. More reliable forecasts several weeks ahead could support agricultural planning, energy management, water allocation and preparations for severe weather. However, subseasonal forecasts remain probabilistic: improving their statistical reliability does not make it possible to predict exact local weather conditions six weeks ahead with certainty. Unlike the recent releases of general-purpose frontier models by Google and Meta, this development concerns a specific scientific advance supported by a peer-reviewed publication and comparative experiments. It illustrates a broader direction in AI research: using machine learning to improve complex scientific prediction systems without necessarily replacing their underlying physical foundations.

## Risk and control {#risk}

### Anthropic finds AI safety gap: models reject harmful requests but can miss coordinated campaigns

On October 14, Anthropic published new findings about protecting Claude against election interference and coordinated political manipulation. Testing conducted with the independent Institute for Strategic Dialogue and McCain Institute revealed an important limitation in current AI safeguards. Claude generally rejected direct requests to assist with deceptive political campaigns, misinformation and other prohibited election-related activities. However, when researchers divided a malicious campaign into many smaller tasks that appeared harmless in isolation, the system did not always recognize the broader operation. This was an observed outcome of targeted adversarial testing, not merely a hypothetical concern. Nevertheless, the finding does not establish that every multistep attack succeeds or that Claude consistently complies with malicious requests. It highlights a distinction between evaluating individual prompts and understanding coordinated activity across an extended workflow.

Independent analysts tested the safeguards in the United States, Israel and Brazil. Their scenarios included direct requests for prohibited assistance and sequences of separate tasks involving audience research, website development, content drafting and distribution planning. Each individual activity may have legitimate uses in journalism, research or civic communication. Combined within a covert influence campaign, however, these tasks can help distribute false information and mislead voters. The challenge is that a safety system assessing each request separately may overlook the overall purpose of a series of operations. Recognizing misuse requires connecting actions across time and examining their relationships and possible objectives. Following the tests, Anthropic introduced additional classifiers designed to detect emerging forms of multistep abuse, including efforts to create fake political accounts and news websites, as well as financially motivated election-related schemes. The company also strengthened protections against election misuse associated with foreign state actors.

Anthropic provided usage data to establish the scale and character of political conversations on Claude. The company analyzed a random sample of approximately 250,000 US Claude.ai consumer conversations from September 21 through September 27, 2026. According to its findings, around 5.75% of conversations broadly concerned politics, public policy or social issues, while 0.51% specifically concerned elections or voting. Requests asking Claude to recommend whom or what to vote for represented approximately 0.01% of conversations. In a separate analysis of a similarly sized sample, requests violating election integrity policies accounted for approximately 0.002%. These figures describe activity observed on a particular consumer service and should not be generalized to the entire AI industry. A low share of explicitly prohibited prompts also does not rule out coordinated misuse, since an organized campaign may consist of numerous apparently legitimate individual requests.

The company's threat investigations identified activity extending beyond controlled experiments. According to Anthropic, foreign-based operators of a fake news website used Claude to produce coverage of the US midterm elections that favored Russian, Iranian and North Korean narratives. The company also observed attempts to generate fabricated political news and related content to attract American audiences and earn advertising revenue. Anthropic banned the accounts involved in these activities. The findings demonstrate attempts to misuse an AI service, but they do not establish that the resulting content reached large audiences or changed electoral outcomes. Anthropic says it has improved its ability to recognize foreign-state election abuse and reinforced cyber protections for election infrastructure and political campaigns. These actions are concrete responses to the identified problems, although the announcement does not prove that the defenses will detect every future coordinated operation.

For the global AI industry, the significance extends beyond elections. A similar safety problem arises in enterprise AI agents that perform extended sequences of actions involving documents, browsers, external services and software tools. Even when individual operations appear permitted, their combination may produce an unauthorized outcome. Effective safeguards must therefore evaluate not only isolated commands but also activity history, accumulated permissions, the origin of information and the consequences of the overall workflow. The October 14 announcement is distinct from Anthropic's October 9 report about unintended Claude actions during evaluations and internal use. This publication focuses on targeted testing against coordinated political misuse, observed real-world abuse and improvements to detection systems. Its central lesson is that protecting increasingly autonomous AI requires understanding the context and intent of multistep activity rather than filtering individual messages alone.

## Where AI becomes product {#applied}

### AI agents account for over half of enterprise ChatGPT output tokens in OpenAI's EU sample

On October 14, OpenAI published The EU’s next chapter in AI is agentic, a research report examining how European companies are adopting AI agents in practical work. OpenAI Economic Research analyzed the transition from conventional chatbot interactions toward tools capable of carrying out multistep tasks. According to the report, Codex and ChatGPT Work account for more than half of output tokens in the examined sample of European enterprise customers. Output tokens measure the volume of material generated by these systems rather than the number of successful business operations. Nevertheless, the finding indicates that agentic tools have become a substantial part of how sampled organizations use ChatGPT. It should not be interpreted as proof that more than half of all enterprise workflows are autonomous, that agents complete most tasks successfully or that corresponding financial benefits have already been realized.

The growth of agentic adoption among smaller organizations is particularly notable. For this analysis, OpenAI defines small and medium businesses as organizations associated with no more than 200 users. Since May 2026, weekly active users of Codex and ChatGPT Work among these businesses in the European Union have increased 8.1 times. Growth occurred across every EU member state, although usage intensity varies considerably. This suggests that agentic tools are not being adopted exclusively by large corporations with substantial technology budgets. Smaller teams are also beginning to use AI for activities involving multiple steps, interaction with working materials and the production of completed outputs. However, rapid relative growth does not disclose the absolute number of additional users or establish how many companies have transferred mission-critical workflows to autonomous systems.

The study also reveals substantial differences between countries. Among the enterprise accounts examined, the share of output tokens generated through Codex and ChatGPT Work ranged from 22% in Slovenia to 85% in Lithuania. These figures are not national adoption rates for the entire AI market, nor do they represent the proportion of citizens using AI agents. They describe selected OpenAI customer accounts. The contrast illustrates how differently organizations employ similar technologies. Some enterprises may still use AI primarily for conversational assistance, while others devote a much larger share of activity to coding and extended task execution. Token-based comparisons nevertheless require caution. Different workloads consume different quantities of tokens, meaning that a higher agentic share does not automatically establish greater employee productivity or better business outcomes.

A separate part of the report examines organizations using AI most intensively. OpenAI defines frontier firms as the top 10% of companies by output tokens per active user, using ChatGPT Enterprise and Business accounts. In September 2026, frontier companies in the EU generated more than four times as many output tokens per active user as typical EU firms in the sample. Even these leading European organizations, however, generated less than half the output-token volume per user of frontier firms outside the EU. EU companies represented approximately 10% of the global frontier-firm group. These differences should not be treated as direct evidence of lower European economic efficiency, because the metric measures usage intensity rather than revenue, profit or labor productivity. They do, however, reveal a considerable gap in the depth of adoption across organizations and regions.

For the global AI industry, the publication provides quantitative evidence of how business use of AI is shifting toward tools designed to complete work rather than simply answer questions. Software developers, integration providers and business consultants may find opportunities in connecting agents to repeatable corporate processes such as document preparation, coding, research and operational analysis. For enterprise leaders, the central challenge is distinguishing tool adoption from measurable value. Useful performance indicators include task completion time, error frequency, operating cost, human correction requirements and compliance with organizational policies. Unlike the October 13 Higgsfield story, which described one company's application of AI to video production, this announcement presents aggregate usage patterns among European business customers of OpenAI. The main conclusion is that agentic AI already represents a meaningful share of activity within the studied EU customer base, while adoption intensity and business outcomes remain highly uneven.

## Architecture and implementation {#stack}

### NVIDIA and Siemens raise AI agent verification scores from 55% to 95% while cutting agent steps by 66%

On October 14, NVIDIA published technical results from a collaboration with Siemens focused on improving the reliability of AI agents used in semiconductor design. Engineers applied the open NVIDIA NeMo Gym library to evaluate and optimize the Siemens Fuse EDA AI Agent. According to the published benchmark, the mean composite verification score increased from approximately 55% to 95%, while the number of agent turns fell from 155 to 53, a reduction of roughly 66%. Output tokens also decreased from approximately 81,000 to 29,000. These are results from a particular engineering evaluation, not evidence that AI agents have become universally 95% reliable. The development belongs to the technical infrastructure of agentic systems: the researchers improved how an existing model interacted with specialized tools rather than introducing a new foundation model.

The engineering problem involved standard-cell characterization, a critical step in designing semiconductor devices. Chip designers require accurate estimates of signal timing, power consumption and circuit behavior under different operating conditions. These characteristics are represented in Liberty files used by electronic design automation software. The Siemens agent coordinated specialized functions in the Solido Characterization Suite, including configuration, transistor-level simulation, processing calculated values and preparing final artifacts. A central reliability problem was that intermediate steps could appear successful even when they contained incorrect thresholds, missing operating conditions or incomplete results. An early configuration mistake could propagate through subsequent stages and eventually produce a technically plausible but unusable final file. Such subtle failures are particularly dangerous in complex engineering workflows because conventional checks of individual tool calls may not detect errors affecting the completed artifact.

To address these weaknesses, the team constructed a controlled evaluation environment with NVIDIA NeMo Gym. Each execution began from a defined state and used a specified collection of tools and verification rules. The final Liberty output was evaluated against five criteria: coverage of expected cells and operating conditions, structural validity, relative correctness against reference artifacts, numerical accuracy and integrity of the completed result. If any mandatory verification condition failed, the composite reward became zero. This prevented partially correct but critically incomplete outputs from receiving misleadingly positive assessments. Engineers examined tool-call trajectories, diagnosed ambiguous instructions and revised the descriptions of functions available to the agent through the Model Context Protocol. They also simplified overlapping file and shell interfaces and introduced explicit JSON schemas for tool arguments. The resulting improvement came from making the agent's working environment clearer and its final outputs easier to verify.

The benchmark used the open GF180MCU semiconductor process design kit. The full dataset comprised 229 standard cells evaluated across 36 process, voltage and temperature conditions. However, individual optimization runs were restricted to two cells across those 36 conditions, creating a smaller representative workload. The team conducted approximately 25 evaluation runs, each requiring around 1.5 to two hours. Claude Opus 4.8 running in Claude Code remained the fixed model and execution environment throughout the comparison. Following revisions to agent instructions and tool descriptions, the mean composite verification score rose from about 55% to 95%. Agent turns decreased by approximately 66%, while generated output tokens fell by roughly 64%. These figures describe the published development benchmark and must not be extrapolated automatically to other models, engineering domains or full-scale production workloads. The results establish an improvement under the selected test conditions rather than a universal guarantee of correctness.

For the wider AI industry, the case demonstrates that improving autonomous-agent performance does not always require adopting a larger or more capable foundation model. Important limitations may lie in tool selection, ambiguous parameter descriptions, incomplete intermediate validation and weak assessment of final outputs. A disciplined evaluation framework can expose these problems and guide targeted improvements. Similar principles could apply to computer-aided engineering, enterprise integration, data analysis and software development, provided each domain has appropriate reference outputs and specialized verification rules. The October 14 publication is distinct from NVIDIA's October 13 open-source release of Run:ai, which concerns scheduling and sharing GPU infrastructure. This new technical report focuses on the reliability and efficiency of agent execution. Its broader lesson is that the infrastructure required for dependable AI consists not only of computing hardware and models, but also of well-defined tools, reproducible evaluations and rigorous checks of completed work.

## Finance {#finance}

### Salience Labs raises $125 million to scale optical switching chips for AI data centers

On October 14, UK-based Salience Labs announced the completion of a $125 million Series B financing round to scale its optical switching technology for artificial intelligence data centers. The Oxford-headquartered company develops photonic components designed to improve communication between computing accelerators. The round was led by Bullhound Capital and included strategic investments from AMD Ventures and Intel Capital. The British Business Bank, the United Kingdom's government-owned economic development bank, separately confirmed that it invested £10 million. Other participating investors included SBI Group, Hudson River Trading and existing shareholders. This is a completed financing round rather than a proposed transaction or an announcement of fundraising negotiations. Its significance lies in directing capital toward the physical infrastructure required to operate advanced AI systems, rather than toward training another general-purpose language model.

Salience Labs addresses an increasingly important engineering challenge in large AI computing clusters: the movement of data between graphics processing units. Modern AI models often depend on many accelerators operating together, exchanging information throughout training and inference. Even when individual processors offer substantial computational performance, network latency and insufficient communication capacity can constrain the throughput of the overall system. Conventional networking infrastructure also consumes electricity and requires supporting electronic components. Salience Labs is developing optical circuit switching technology that uses light to establish high-speed communication paths within data center infrastructure. The company claims that this approach can reduce network latency, energy consumption and costs while improving the effective performance of AI workloads. These remain technology and commercial claims; the financing announcement does not provide independent, production-scale benchmark results establishing specific savings across customer deployments.

The company's technology originates from research associated with the University of Oxford and the University of Münster. Founded in 2021, Salience Labs specializes in integrated photonic solutions for data center networking. Optical switching is intended to create efficient communication paths between computing resources while reducing dependence on conventional electronic switching for every stage of transmission. Such capabilities could become particularly valuable in large clusters where many processors must coordinate their work. CEO and co-founder Vaysh Kewada emphasizes the importance of producing fully integrated, on-chip optical switching solutions at commercial scale. Unlike an optimization software package that can be distributed online, this technology requires semiconductor manufacturing, hardware validation, integration into networking systems and compatibility with established data center architectures. Those factors create substantial engineering and commercialization requirements even after research prototypes demonstrate promising results.

Salience Labs intends to use the new financing to accelerate deployment of its optical circuit switching technology, expand high-volume manufacturing and strengthen its presence in the United States. Strategic backing from AMD Ventures and Intel Capital indicates interest from major semiconductor industry participants in improving connectivity around AI accelerators. However, their investment does not by itself establish that AMD or Intel have signed binding large-scale procurement agreements for Salience Labs products. Similarly, the British Business Bank's participation confirms public-backed investment without guaranteeing eventual commercial success. Important next steps include demonstrating performance in representative customer environments, establishing manufacturing yields, verifying reliability and proving attractive costs at scale. The announcement does not disclose a new company valuation, current revenue, profitability or a quantified order backlog. Consequently, the investment amount should not be interpreted as evidence that Salience Labs has already achieved mass-market adoption.

For the global AI economy, the transaction illustrates how investor attention is extending beyond the accelerators themselves to the infrastructure connecting them. As computing clusters expand, networking efficiency increasingly influences the cost of training and serving large models. Additional processing capacity may deliver diminishing returns if processors spend substantial time waiting for data transfers. Optical switching could help address part of this bottleneck, although any commercial advantage must be measured under real workloads and operating conditions. The development is distinct from NVIDIA's October 13 decision to open-source Run:ai: that platform addresses software scheduling and allocation of GPU resources, while Salience Labs develops physical components for communication between computing devices. The central financial conclusion is that investors are willing to commit substantial capital to specialized hardware technologies intended to improve AI infrastructure efficiency without relying exclusively on purchasing more accelerators.

## Business opportunities {#monetization}

### Verifiable AI agents for engineering and enterprise workflows
The NVIDIA and Siemens case suggests a practical business opportunity: improving the reliability of existing AI agents without developing a new foundation model. In one engineering evaluation, the reported composite verification score increased from approximately 55% to 95%, while agent turns decreased by 66%. The improvement came from clearer instructions, better tool descriptions and rigorous validation of final outputs. For a smaller systems integrator, this approach is more attainable than building a universal autonomous platform. Enterprises need focused systems that perform a defined set of operations correctly and produce evidence that the result can be trusted. Potential customers include manufacturers, enterprise software developers, engineering companies and organizations operating repetitive technical workflows.

An initial service could focus on one existing business process. For example, an agent might examine logs from an integration between two enterprise applications, identify a likely cause of failure and prepare recommendations for an engineer. The provider would assemble 50–100 previously resolved cases with known outcomes. Each case would have explicit acceptance criteria, such as identifying the correct error, finding the relevant document or satisfying required validation rules. The implementation would record tool calls, intermediate actions, errors and completed outputs. Engineers could then refine prompts, simplify overlapping interfaces, introduce structured tool arguments and add independent checks. Sensitive operations would remain subject to authorization and human approval. The purpose is to make an existing workflow more dependable, not grant the agent unlimited control.

Commercial value should be measured through successful task completion, incorrect actions, execution time, token consumption and the number of human interventions. A first offering could be a paid agent assessment, followed by an improvement project and periodic regression testing after changes to models or tools. Testing must also consider sequences of individually acceptable actions that collectively create an unauthorized result, a weakness highlighted by Anthropic's research. Effective governance therefore requires evaluating the complete workflow as well as individual commands. This remains a proposed service rather than an established software product. The Siemens benchmark describes a specific engineering environment and does not guarantee equivalent improvements in unrelated customer workloads.

### Improving industry forecasts without replacing existing models
The Microsoft Research study published in Nature demonstrates that machine learning can improve forecasts by correcting systematic errors in established prediction systems. Researchers applied probabilistic bias correction to weather predictions two to six weeks ahead and reported improvements across a large share of evaluated targets. The commercially interesting principle is not the ability to predict exact weather conditions far in advance. It is the possibility of extracting additional value from an existing forecasting system by studying its historical errors. Similar approaches could be investigated for inventory demand, electricity consumption, service capacity and warehouse replenishment. However, adapting findings from atmospheric science to business data requires separate development, testing and validation. The meteorological results cannot be presented as evidence of effectiveness in commercial forecasting.

A first pilot could use historical forecasts produced by one company. For example, a distributor may routinely estimate demand for particular products four weeks ahead and later compare those estimates with realized sales. The provider would collect archived forecasts and corresponding actual results, investigate missing data and identify recurring prediction errors. A correction model could then accept the original forecast and return an adjustment together with an uncertainty estimate. The existing forecasting procedure must remain available as a baseline. Evaluation should use later time periods that were not included in training, allowing a fair comparison of corrected and uncorrected forecasts. Seasonal demand, changes in product assortment, promotions and periods of unusual market behavior should be evaluated separately because historical patterns may not remain stable.

Success could be measured through lower forecast errors, fewer stockouts, reduced excess inventory and more reliable uncertainty estimates. Financial benefits should include the costs of data preparation, integration and ongoing maintenance. Rather than replacing a customer's entire planning system, the service could operate as an additional analytical layer connected to an existing report or business application. A sensible initial commercial offer would be a paid diagnostic study. If historical forecasts do not contain sufficiently stable and correctable errors, deploying a new model may not justify its cost. This is an experimental business opportunity rather than an existing validated product. Microsoft Research demonstrated improvements in weather forecasting, not guaranteed gains in retail, logistics or industrial planning.

## Conclusions
1. Microsoft Research demonstrated that AI can improve scientific forecasts by correcting systematic errors in established models. The results concern probabilistic weather forecasts two to six weeks ahead. They do not establish the ability to predict exact local conditions without error six weeks into the future.
2. Anthropic identified a weakness in evaluating prompts individually: a sequence of apparently acceptable actions can conceal coordinated misuse. Defenses for autonomous agents need to consider activity history and the overall objective. Detected political manipulation attempts do not establish an impact on election outcomes.
3. OpenAI's data indicates substantial adoption of Codex and ChatGPT Work among the European enterprises examined. More than half of output tokens in the sample came from these tools. However, generation intensity does not equal the proportion of automated workflows or demonstrate equivalent productivity improvements.
4. NVIDIA and Siemens showed that engineering-agent performance can improve without replacing the underlying model. In a specific evaluation, the verification score rose from approximately 55% to 95%, while agent turns fell by 66%. These results are not universal performance characteristics of autonomous systems.
5. Salience Labs raised $125 million to develop optical switching technology for AI infrastructure. The investment highlights interest in reducing communication bottlenecks between accelerators. Strategic investors' participation does not establish mass-market orders or profitability.
6. Conga expanded connections between external AI assistants and contract, pricing and quotation systems through MCP. The development brings intelligent functions into established business workflows. However, access to enterprise data requires permission enforcement, information verification and approval of consequential actions.
